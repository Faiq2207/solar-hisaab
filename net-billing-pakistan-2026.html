Net billing in Pakistan 2026, explained simply

Prices updated 11 November 2026

In February 2026, NEPRA replaced net metering with net billing for new solar connections. Here’s what changed and what it means for your savings.

What changed

Under the old net metering system, every unit you sent to the grid cancelled out a unit you bought, at the same price. Under the new NEPRA (Prosumer) Regulations 2026, buying and selling have different prices. You still pay the full tariff for units you take from the grid, but extra units you send back are bought at around the national average energy purchase price, about Rs 11 per unit. Before, exported units were credited at roughly Rs 22–27.

Who is affected

New solar users are on net billing. Net metering applications submitted up to 8 February 2026 are processed under the old rules, and existing net metering contracts stay protected until they expire.

What it means for your money

Say your system sends 150 extra units to the grid in a month. Under net billing that earns about Rs 1,650. If you used those same units at home instead of buying them at around Rs 35 each, you’d save about Rs 5,250. So the new rule rewards using your own solar power: run heavy appliances during the day, size your system to your real use, and consider batteries for the evening.

Is solar still worth it?

For most homes, yes, if the system is sized to what you actually use. Units you use yourself save the full bill rate, which is often Rs 30–45 with taxes. The calculator on the home page uses the new export rate in its savings estimate.

Common questions

What is net billing in Pakistan?

Net billing is the system NEPRA introduced in February 2026. You pay the normal tariff for grid units and get about Rs 11 per unit for extra solar power you export.

Is net metering finished in Pakistan?

For new users, yes. Applications submitted up to 8 February 2026 follow the old net metering rules, and existing contracts are protected until they expire.

Is solar still worth it after net billing?

Yes for most homes, as long as you use most of the power yourself, because every unit you don’t buy saves your full bill rate.
